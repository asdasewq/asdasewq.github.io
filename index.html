Abstract

Text-to-audio (TTA) diffusion models have made high-quality audio synthesis more accessible through web platforms. Their growing adoption also raises concerns about audio misinformation and copyright infringement. While in-generation watermarking offers a proactive mechanism for provenance attribution by embedding identifiers during synthesis, existing schemes remain constrained by fundamental trade-offs: they either require training auxiliary neural networks, suffer from lossy mel-spectrogram conversions, or rely on stored reference signals and candidate-guided trajectory optimization that fail to scale for blind attribution.

To address them, we propose NoiseSeal, a novel training-free framework for audio provenance attribution in TTA diffusion models. It embeds a Polar-coded, cryptographically whitened multi-bit watermark into the initial noise using Pairwise Coordinate Swaps. These swaps enforce target orderings between disjoint channel pairs within each latent token while preserving the multiset of noise values in that token. Watermark recovery combines blind inversion with Polar decoding and requires neither stored source audio nor an initial-noise reference. Experiments on Stable Audio 3 (SA) show that NoiseSeal maintains audio fidelity and text-audio alignment. Under clean conditions, it achieves bit accuracy above 98%. Comparisons with five audio watermarking baselines across ten attacks further demonstrate promising robustness.
